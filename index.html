Pull up a stool

Right in the middle of Eastport's antique row sits a little house that's been feeding people since 1922 — first as a soda fountain, and as a luncheonette since 1956. Walk in and it feels less like a restaurant and more like sitting down in a family member's kitchen.

Grab one of the spinning stools at the long counter and you've got a front-row seat to Keith working the grill, orders flying, and coffee coming in giant mugs. Rather have a table? There's room for that too, and on a nice day the fenced-in patio has picnic tables under the umbrellas.

There's a sign on the wall warning you not to let the regulars scare you away. Don't. Come in once and there's a good chance you'll end up one of them.

Good home cooking and a little bit of craziness. Same as it ever was.

Love Found at the Counter

Everyone who comes here to work becomes family — and for the Glynn boys, that turned out to be literally true.

Keith & Kristin

Noreen hired Kristin as a waitress. Keith was behind the counter. The rest is history — and Keith is still at the grill today, carrying on his mom's legacy.

Patrick & Lindsay

Patrick had been working in and out of the luncheonette since he was 13. Lindsay came on board to work — and found more than a job.

Three sons, seven grandkids, and a counter that keeps bringing people together.
